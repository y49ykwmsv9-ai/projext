# Chronicle: A World in Motion

## Opening
The documentary begins with a world map resolving from darkness. Borders, dates and regional layers appear in sequence. The narration explains that the map is an analytical interface, while the underlying historical claims remain tied to sources.

## Geography
The camera moves from the world to regions and cities. Country borders are only one layer of geography. Regional ownership, control, settlement and transport networks can operate on different scales and different clocks.

## Population and economy
Population is presented as a changing system rather than a static number. Economic capacity is likewise shown as the interaction of production, taxation, debt, inflation, trade and industrial capacity. Where a value is simulated, the narration explicitly identifies it as simulation output.

## War as a system
Mobilization becomes a process of manpower, equipment, readiness and time. Front lines are shown together with reserves, depots, industrial centers and supply routes. The visual language emphasizes relationships rather than spectacle.

## Alternate history
A baseline timeline is compared with an alternate simulation. The first divergence is highlighted, followed by the events that depend on it. The documentary avoids claiming that a counterfactual outcome was inevitable.

## Inside Chronicle
A natural-language command becomes a structured state change. Other actors receive their own decision cycles. Reactions become events, events become historical records, and later effects remain connected to their origins.

## Closing
The final map returns to the historical timeline. Simulation layers fade while source labels remain. The closing argument is that a model becomes more useful when its assumptions, evidence and uncertainty remain visible.
